# Starting a Social Media Career in Nigeria: Why It's Hard and How Beginners Break In

*By Isaac Favour Chimdinma*

Starting a **social media career in Nigeria** looks easy from the outside. You learn Canva, you learn to edit videos, you finish a few courses, and you apply. Then the reality shows up: almost every job asks for experience you don't have yet.

I'm a recent graduate building my skills in social media management, and I've spent weeks applying for roles. This article is about what I've noticed, and what I'm doing about it. It's my own experience, not a rule for every employer, and I hope it helps other beginners feel less alone.

## The experience trap

The first problem is simple: you need experience to get a job, and you need a job to get experience.

In my search, I've seen listings for entry-level social media roles that ask for two or three years of experience. Some of them offer pay that is far below what that experience would normally earn. For someone starting out, this feels confusing. If the role is entry level, why does it need years of experience?

## Skills versus trust

You can have the skills and still not get the job.

I can design posts in Canva, edit short videos in CapCut, write SEO content and plan a content calendar. I also have a HubSpot Academy social media marketing certificate. But skills on paper are not the same as proof in an employer's eyes.

From what I've seen, many employers trust someone with a long work history more than a fresh graduate, even when the graduate can do the same work. I understand the reasoning, because hiring is a risk. But it leaves talented beginners stuck outside the door.

## The underpayment problem

There is another pattern that many beginners talk about quietly.

Sometimes a company says it wants a fresh graduate, but the real reason is a low salary. The person hired may end up handling a full workload (content, design, replies, reports) while being paid very little because they are "still learning".

Beginners should learn, and a first job is not always the best-paid job. But there is a difference between a fair entry-level salary and being underpaid for work that deserves more. Knowing your value helps you tell the two apart.

## How I'm building experience without a job

Waiting for someone to give me experience was not working, so I started creating my own proof of work.

- **I grew my own Snapchat account.** It has reached 318 followers, with growth of 156.5% in 28 days, using short videos edited in CapCut. I checked which stories performed best and used that to plan more content.
- **I built a sample content plan.** I made a 30-day social media content plan for a fictional bakery and published it on GitHub, so anyone can read my planning process.
- **I made a Canva portfolio.** It shows my designs, my writing samples and the results of my work.
- **I keep learning.** I'm studying through HubSpot Academy and practising new tools.

None of this replaces a real job, but it turns "I have skills" into "here is what I have done".

## What beginners can do

If you're in the same position, here is what has helped me think clearly:

1. **Build proof of work.** Run your own page, make sample projects and keep a portfolio. Employers can see results far better than they can read a list of skills.
2. **Know your minimum.** Decide the lowest pay you will accept before you apply, so you aren't pressured in the moment.
3. **Start small.** Help a small business or a friend's brand for a low fee in exchange for a testimonial. Real clients are real experience.
4. **Apply anyway.** A job listing is a wish list, not a strict rule. If you meet most of the requirements, apply and be honest about what you have.
5. **Network.** Connect with people in the field, comment on their posts, and share your work. Many opportunities come through people.

## Conclusion

Building a **social media career in Nigeria** as a beginner is hard, mostly because of the experience gap and the low pay. But it isn't impossible. The beginners who move forward are the ones who create their own proof, know their worth and keep applying.
